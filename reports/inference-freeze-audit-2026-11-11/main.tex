\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.} Both MLIP-research and TiAl exported DPA4 checkpoints to
\texttt{.pt2}, and actual native LAMMPS calculations consumed these exports.
However, several baseline, fine-tuned prediction and phonon workflows continued
to use the original \texttt{.pt} checkpoint. Their launchers disabled inference
compilation and optional Triton kernels. Thus export was implemented, but was not
consistently used for repeated inference. The matched A100 benchmark now confirms
2.04--4.60-fold speedup for DPA4-Mini with standard freeze, 2.83--5.19-fold with
Triton2 freeze, and 1.55--3.19-fold for DPA4-Neo with standard freeze in the
initial job. The completed Neo retry gives 2.78--4.56-fold speedup with Triton2;
on TiAl216/432/864 it is 1.59--1.61 times faster than GRACE-2L fp32 and
1.68--2.06 times faster than GRACE-2L fp64. Every completed export passed the
predefined energy, force and stress tolerances. These are matched ASE timings,
not a comparison at equal DFT accuracy.

\section*{Direct evidence}
In MLIP-research, job 20950326 ran
\texttt{dp --pt freeze -c model.ckpt-24.pt -o exported}, exited successfully,
and created \texttt{exported.pt2}; export took 242.12 seconds. This is
one-time compilation cost, not inference latency. The export hash is
\path{772b5398c4edaf67859d354ca652868c5a941d3b94973ed70ad47ec684d81988}.
Consume-only job 20950337 used this same file in native LAMMPS on A100 and passed
energy, force and stress equivalence checks. In contrast, the original-model
Al32 baseline and fine-tuned evaluation workers constructed
\texttt{DeepPot(checkpoint)} from \texttt{.pt}.

In TiAl, the original DPA4-Neo CPU export passed equivalence checks
(\texttt{baseline/export\_checks.json}). The later
\texttt{evaluate\_pilot.py} creates a \texttt{.pt2}, compares it on probes,
executes \texttt{del export}, and evaluates train/validation structures and
harmonic forces using the \texttt{.pt} object. Separately, the Ti melting
workflow did use \texttt{model\_15000.pt2} in LAMMPS. Its A100 preflight,
job 20961138, records the consumed model hash and successful equivalence checks.
These findings are scoped to inspected workflows, rather than all historical jobs.

The inspected exports set \texttt{DP\_TRITON\_INFER=0}.
This still gives AOTInductor compilation; it does not mean that a frozen model
runs eagerly. The
\href{https://docs.deepmodeling.com/projects/deepmd/en/v3.2.0/model/dpa4.html}{official DPA4 documentation}
states that export captures the inference settings in the executable graph.
The
\href{https://huggingface.co/deepmodelingcommunity/DPA4-OMat24}{upstream model card}
recommends testing Triton level 2, with full float32 accumulation. The archive is
target-specific. Changing environment variables after loading an existing
export does not rebuild it.

These are post-hoc timings on different train geometries with no repeated

was bypassed. Each precision used two fresh processes, five warmup calls and

\section*{Matched experiment completed}
Run \texttt{inference-freeze-audit-20261111-01}, Slurm job
\textbf{20992566}, was submitted after explicit user approval.
Allocation: one A100, four CPUs, 32 GiB, account \texttt{imet-2026-2},
partition \texttt{v100}, maximum 45 minutes (0.75 GPU-hours and 3 CPU-hours).
The job completed successfully on 11 November, 21:22:48--21:52:26 local time
(UTC+5), earlier than its queue estimate. Actual allocation cost was 1778
GPU-seconds (0.4939 GPU-hours) and 7112 CPU-seconds (1.9756 CPU-hours).
Eight cases completed both measurement processes. One export exceeded its
420-second subprocess limit; this failure is preserved despite the Slurm exit code 0.

Nine cases compare DPA4-Mini and DPA4-Neo in eager \texttt{.pt},
\texttt{.pt2}/Triton0 and \texttt{.pt2}/Triton2 modes, GRACE-1L fp64,
and GRACE-2L fp32/fp64. All cases use identical Ti96 and TiAl216/432/864
input bytes and identical small deterministic perturbations. The complete
case order is reversed for the second fresh-process block. Each size has
five warmups and 30 synchronized full ASE energy/force/stress calls per block.
Cold load, first-call and export costs are recorded separately.
The initial models are fixed; there is no new training or DFT evaluation.

Serialization gates, fixed before submission, are maximum differences of
$10^{-5}$ eV/atom in energy, $2\,10^{-4}$ eV/\AA\ in force components, and
$10^{-6}$ eV/\AA$^3$ in tensile ASE stress. Speed claims for an export must be
accompanied by these checks. The experiment does not establish equal DFT accuracy,
long-time MD stability, or native LAMMPS/Kokkos throughput.
