\section{Scope and protocol}
These are software checks of the previously fine-tuned DPA4-Mini-OMat24-v20260805
and GRACE-1L-OMAT models. Inputs and labels remain synthetic, not DFT; the
checkpoints are not scientific teachers. The source run is
\path{runs/fm-finetune-20261017-01/assessment.json}.
Each model continues from its checkpoint after 20 optimizer updates.
Original files and installed training environments are not modified.

The restart audit snapshots the state after loading and before the next update,
compares model parameters and Adam tensors with the source checkpoint, and checks
the counters. Accepted continuations reach Adam update 24. The stopping target
is extended, so the learning-rate schedule is evaluated for the extended budget;
this is not a claim of bitwise equivalence to an uninterrupted training run.

Export comparisons use the \emph{same final checkpoint24} on both sides, not
the model before versus after optimization. Four periodic Al4 structures are
used: two original synthetic validation geometries, a sheared/scaled triclinic
cell, and a smaller cell with periodic-image neighbours. The geometry and its
SHA-256 are retained with predictions. Energies are total eV, forces eV/\AA,
and tensile stresses eV/\AA$^3$, Voigt $xx,yy,zz,yz,xz,xy$.
LAMMPS uses \texttt{units metal}; zero velocities remove kinetic pressure.
Stress is minus its pressure tensor converted from bar using ASE's unit constant.
Small differences in legacy pressure conversion constants can therefore remain.

Each comparison requires
$|y_{\rm export}-y_{\rm checkpoint}|\leq a+r|y_{\rm checkpoint}|$.
The tolerances were fixed in source before submitting the first GPU job:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Model & $a_E$ (eV) & $a_F$ (eV/\AA) & $a_\sigma$ (eV/\AA$^3$) & $r$ \\
\midrule
DPA4 & $10^{-4}$ & $10^{-4}$ & $10^{-6}$ & $10^{-4}$ \\
GRACE & $10^{-7}$ & $10^{-7}$ & $10^{-9}$ & $10^{-7}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
No tolerances were relaxed after observing results. Comparisons check finite
values and shapes as well as numerical agreement. LAMMPS performs \texttt{run 0}
and writes atom-ID-sorted forces; this is not an MD stability or speed benchmark.

\section{DPA4 restart and export}
The native DeepMD 3.2.0 restart was first tested in job 20950321. Its source
checkpoint has Adam iteration 20 but stores a zero-based completed loop index 19.
The installed trainer uses that value directly as the next loop start, causing
the last displayed batch number to repeat. The first check rejected that counter
behaviour and retained the run, rather than accepting a false step-20 assertion.

Job \textbf{20950322} used a guarded compatibility adapter in
\path{scripts/hpc/restart-child.py}. If all restored Adam iteration counters agree
and exceed the saved loop index by exactly one, it advances the next loop start
to the Adam count and rebuilds the scheduler. It accepts no other discrepancy.
The installed DeepMD package and source checkpoint are unchanged. Exact model
and optimizer restoration was verified (413 tensors, including 267 Adam tensors);
the continuation performed four updates
and ended at Adam iteration 24 / internal completed index 23. The changed
scheduler learning rate is recorded explicitly. This adapter is validated for
the tested DeepMD 3.2.0 Adam setup, not every backend or optimizer.

The first AOTInductor export then failed because \texttt{CUDA\_HOME} was unset.
Job \textbf{20950326} reuses the hashed checkpoint24 and cache from job 20950322;
it does not train again. Its toolchain explicitly selects
\path{/opt/cuda-13.0} and GCC toolset 12. Export uses the target A100 and float32
with inference TF32, AMP and optional custom Triton approximations disabled.
The upstream
\href{https://docs.deepmodeling.com/projects/deepmd/en/stable/model/dpa4.html}{DPA4 export documentation}
specifies the AOTInductor \texttt{.pt2} format for ASE and LAMMPS.
The export and ASE checks passed in job 20950326. Maximum differences were
$9.54\,10^{-7}$~eV in energy, $5.63\,10^{-6}$~eV/\AA\ in force and
$2.32\,10^{-8}$~eV/\AA$^3$ in stress, within the preset tolerances.
The bundled pip LAMMPS binary then exited with SIGSEGV before reading its input.
CPU diagnostic jobs 20950328 and 20950330 reproduced a pre-main startup crash,
including with an alternative Intel MPI runtime. The precise underlying binary
failure was not established; this is not evidence of an exported-model error.
An official LAMMPS 22Jul2025 Update2 build was prepared from commit
\path{a33449868448baf3c73d8eacdb2d329b13361696} with GCC 12. Its serial variant
started normally but could not load the installed DeepMD plugin, which requires
\texttt{MPI\_Allgather}. A separate Intel MPI 2021.17 build resolved that
requirement. Both source and binary variants are retained; installed pip files
were not replaced. CPU build jobs 20950331 and 20950333 took 123 and 106 seconds
respectively, with eight CPUs each.

Native loading also required explicit paths to \texttt{torch/lib} and the
Torch CUDA 13 libraries, plus the real CUDA 12.8 runtime required by DeepMD's
native CUDA operations. CUDA major versions were not aliased or substituted.
The accepted activation recipe is \path{scripts/hpc/deepmd-lammps-env.sh}.
Failed consume-only jobs 20950332 (missing MPI symbol), 20950335 (runtime library
paths) and 20950336 (GPU observation interval too coarse) are retained.

Final consume-only job \textbf{20950337} completed in \textbf{32 seconds}, using
one MPI rank and one A100. It did not train or export again. All four native
LAMMPS processes were observed on GPU, with 12 NVIDIA samples each. A two-second
hold after each evaluation made context observation reliable; it adds no force
evaluation or MD step. Reported validation walltimes are not performance data.

\section{LAMMPS software, resources and persistence}
A private GRACE-enabled LAMMPS was compiled from upstream revision
\path{b1f4a6c8cc4e0d0fc93d2dec3a1fcc83558062d4}, following the
\href{https://gracemaker.readthedocs.io/en/latest/gracemaker/install/}{GRACE installation instructions}.
CPU job 20950320 completed on \texttt{apollo23} in 137 seconds using eight CPUs,
32~GiB and a 45-minute cap. The build uses GCC 8.5, ML-PACE, OpenMP, TensorFlow
2.20.0 from the locked GRACE environment, and no MPI. LAMMPS reports 30 March 2026.
The serial host process offloads GRACE evaluations to GPU through TensorFlow;
Kokkos and multi-rank operation were not tested.

The private prefix is
\path{/home6/u3129/mlip-research/software/lammps-grace-b1f4a6c8cc4e}.
Its pinned source archive, compiled executable and shared libraries have an
actual archive in main home and a verified workstation copy; member hashes are
in \path{runs/grace-lammps-build-20261017-01/artifact-backup.json}.

All GPU jobs use partition \texttt{v100}, account \texttt{imet-2026-2}, QoS
\texttt{normal}, one full A100, four CPUs, 32~GiB and a 20-minute cap each.
Every submission records input hashes, source code commit and scheduler ID.
Per-job durations, accepted assessment and export hashes accompany the run
records under \path{runs/fm-deployment-20261017-*/}.
All seven GPU attempts consumed \textbf{606 single-GPU seconds} in total,
including failed attempts, compilation and observation holds. The three CPU
builds plus two diagnostic allocations consumed 2934 allocated CPU-seconds.
Models, exports, restoration snapshots and raw LAMMPS inputs/outputs are backed
up privately in cluster main home and on the workstation, with verified member
checksums. Git stores scripts, configuration, small curated text records and
this source report. Institutional retention and a shared archive remain open.
The accepted component-to-run mapping is
\path{runs/fm-deployment-20261017-07/assessment.json}; earlier failed aggregate
job statuses are not overwritten by this assessment.

The tests do not establish DFT accuracy, physical transferability, long-trajectory
stability, multi-GPU correctness or production performance. The AOTInductor
artifact is tied to its tested hardware/toolchain; revalidate on another target.
